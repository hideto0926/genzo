How it works

The real darkroom workflow.

The photos you take become negatives, then pass through the enlarger, developer and fixer to become a single print. How long you expose, even the temperature that day — it all shows in the result.

Shoot

Choose 35mm, medium or large format film and shoot. Your photos are recorded as negatives.

Expose

Set focus and magnification on the enlarger, then shine light to print onto paper.

Develop

Into the developer under the red safelight. Today's temperature changes how fast it develops.

Fix & Wash

Into the fixer while watching the clock. Lift it out too early and the print turns cloudy.

Features

Shaping light with your hands.

Dodge with your hand

Lay your iPhone face up and hold your hand over the front camera — its shadow falls on the paper. Bring a face out of the shadows by hand.

Three film formats

35mm, medium format 6×6 / 6×7 and large format 4×5. Larger film means finer grain and smoother tones.

Today's weather, in the tray

The day's air temperature becomes the developer temperature. On cold days the image appears slowly; on hot days, all at once.

A critique for every print

Review exposure, focus, development and fixing after each print, and make the next one better.
